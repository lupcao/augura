Accessibility

Updated November 5, 2026

Augura is built to work with the accessibility features of iPhone and Apple Watch. This page says what works on each device, which switches Augura has of its own, and where it does not work well yet. Where something is partly done, we say so instead of leaving it out. If you find a problem, please write to us.

What the common tasks are

When this page says "the common tasks", it means the things most people do in Augura. On iPhone: flip a coin, roll dice, spin the wheel with your own options, draw a tarot card and read it, open History, and use a widget. On Apple Watch: Flip, Ask and Roll. The other modes (party games, the Discover, World and Culture finds) are described under "Known gaps" below.

iPhone

  • VoiceOver. Every draw announces its result in words (a rune is read as its name, a playing card as its card name, a color as "Steel blue, #4682B4"). Buttons and icons have names, decorative icons are hidden, readings use headings (each tarot card, each section of an I Ching reading, wiki and guide pages), and the I Ching hexagram is read line by line. Two-finger double tap (Magic Tap) plays and pauses a song preview, a Picture video, a film trailer, Radio and Podcast. Modes on the Home screen can be arranged with "Move up" and "Move down" actions, so no dragging is needed.
  • Voice Control and Full Keyboard Access. Icon buttons also answer to short names such as "Tap Share", "Tap Settings", "Tap Info" and "Tap Next mode", and a button with a visible word is called by that word.
  • Dark interface. Augura is dark by design, in the app and in its widgets.
  • Not only color. Results are shown with words, digits or shapes (Heads and Tails, Yes and No, dice numbers, the names on the wheel, tarot card names). Color results show a color name. Held dice and chosen tiles in the dice games are marked by shape and state, not only by color.
  • Reduce Motion. The system setting stops the coin, orb, dice and shuffle animations, shows the result at once, keeps guide diagrams still, starts Picture videos paused, and lets the widget dial jump to its new place. Augura's own switch (below) does the same.
  • Other system settings. Dim Flashing Lights calms shuffles (and Color mode never flashes for anyone). Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast give mode screens solid panels. Button Shapes adds outlines or underlines to text-like buttons. Smart Invert leaves card art, photos, 3D objects, videos and the color mode true to their colors. Closed Captions + SDH turns captions on in YouTube videos.
  • Keyboards. With a hardware keyboard, Return draws on a mode screen.
  • Assistive Access (iOS 26 and later). Augura offers a simple version with four large screens: Coin, Dice, Number, and Yes or No.
  • Widgets and Controls say their result in words, and follow your text size where there is room.

Apple Watch

  • VoiceOver. Flip, Ask and Roll announce their result, and you can change the dice count by swiping. The tarot and rune faces read once and cleanly, the Today screen has headings, and the Today complication has a spoken label.
  • AssistiveTouch. Every draw screen has a Quick Action, and the dice count has on-screen − and + buttons.
  • Dark interface. The Watch app is dark in Flip, Ask and Roll.
  • Not only color. Flip, Ask and Roll show words (Heads, Tails, Yes, No) and digits.
  • Reduce Motion. With the setting on, the coin does not animate.
  • Haptics. The Watch follows the Haptics switch you set on your iPhone.

Augura's own switches

Open Settings > Feel in the app:

  • Reduce motion. Stops the same motion as the system setting, even if the system setting is off.
  • Haptics. Turns the taps and buzzes on or off, on the iPhone and on the Apple Watch.
  • Shake to draw. On by default. Switch it off and shaking the phone never draws, so a tremor or a bump cannot cause an accidental draw. The draw buttons keep working.

Known gaps

We would rather tell you than have you find out:

  • Captions and audio descriptions. Augura makes no video of its own. Video and audio come from other people: YouTube, film trailers, podcasts, radio, song previews, audiobooks and your own videos in Picture. Very few of them have captions or audio descriptions. YouTube captions follow your system setting, and a podcast shows the creator's transcript only when its feed offers one. We do not claim Captions or Audio Descriptions.
  • 3D and drawn views. The coin, dice, runes and some game boards are 3D or drawn pictures. The result is also given as text, but the picture itself has little for VoiceOver to explore.
  • WikiRace. The race shows real Wikipedia pages in a web view. Their layout, contrast and VoiceOver support are Wikipedia's, not ours.
  • Timed party games. Heads up, and Letters and categories, run a timer. You can change the round length, or stop the round early, but the games are built around the clock.
  • Apple Watch Chess960. The Chess960 board is a small drawn picture. The back rank and the position code are given as text next to it.
  • Voice Control on Apple Watch. Apple does not offer Voice Control for Apple Watch apps, so Augura on the Watch cannot be used by voice that way.
  • Text size and contrast. Results and reading text follow your text size on the common tasks, but a few fixed-size labels (the Home page tabs, the draws counter, some drawn results) do not grow fully, and some small hint text on the colored backgrounds has less contrast than we want. We are working on both, and we do not claim Sufficient Contrast.
  • Motion that is still left. With Reduce Motion on, the I Ching cast still pauses briefly between lines, the Geomancy chart still fills in over a few seconds, and Ogham spreads reveal their staves one after another.

Feedback

If something in Augura does not work with the way you use your iPhone or Apple Watch, write to support@augura.fyi, or use Send feedback in the app's Settings. Tell us which device and which accessibility setting you use, and which screen. Your email reaches the developer's inbox; see the Privacy Policy for how it is handled.
