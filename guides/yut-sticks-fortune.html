Yut sticks fortune (yutjeom)

Throw four wooden sticks three times and read the traditional Korean New Year phrase the throws spell: yutjeom, a gentle family custom. This page has a free browser tool at the top and a guide to the Yut sticks mode in the Augura iPhone app. The browser tool is simpler than the app mode: there is no web version of the full mode.

Throw the yut sticks here

What it is

Yutjeom (윷점) is a Korean New Year custom. A family throws four half-round wooden yut sticks three times and reads a short, old four-character phrase that goes with the three results. Augura throws four 3D sticks for you, three times, then shows the phrase in Hanja (the Chinese characters used in Korea), its Hangul reading, a short line in Korean and English, and a gentle thought to reflect on.

How to use it

Hold a wish or question quietly in mind, or just enjoy the throw. Tap Throw the sticks, tap the sticks or shake your phone. The sticks fly three times; tap during the throws to settle them at once. With Reduce Motion on, the sticks simply rest and the answer appears. Throw again gives a new sign. Share sends the phrase and its lines as a card.

How the throws count

After each throw, count the sticks that land flat side up. One is do (도, 1), two is gae (개, 2), three is geol (걸, 3). Four flat sticks is yut (윷) and none flat is mo (모); both count as 4. Three throws give three numbers from 1 to 4, for example 1-2-3, and so one of 4 × 4 × 4 = 64 signs.

The odds

Each stick lands flat or round side up with equal chance, chosen by your phone's secure random generator before any stick moves. So the throws behave like real sticks: gae is the most common (6 in 16), do and geol are less common (4 in 16 each), and yut or mo together come up 2 in 16 throws. That is why the 64 signs are not equally likely, although every one of them can appear. The animation, skipping it and reading the result never change what was drawn.

How to read it

Treat the phrase as an image to think about, not as a prediction. Every phrase is centuries old and speaks in pictures of nature and home: a candle in the dark, a crane rising, a traveler finding the road. The English and Korean lines and the reflection were written for Augura in a gentle voice; some phrases that sound harsh in their old wording are rendered softly. Nothing here knows the future. History keeps the throws and the sign exactly as they were shown.

A note on respect

Yutjeom is a living family custom. Use the Korean names (도, 개, 걸, 윷, 모) and the Hanja as given. The Korean wording of the lines was written for Augura and may differ from the explanations printed in old booklets.

Where it comes from

Background

Yut sticks are best known from the board game yutnori, which Korean families play at Seollal, the Lunar New Year. Old Korean books such as the Gyeongdo japji (18th century) and the Dongguk sesigi (1849) describe New Year customs, and printed yutjeom booklets, kept for example by the National Hangeul Museum, list the 64 phrases. Women and children took part in the custom widely. It needs no practitioner and is not a religious rite: it is a playful way to start the year with a thought.

What it cannot do

  • It is not the board game yutnori. There is no board, no tokens, no rules and no online match here.
  • The browser tool throws no 3D sticks and has no shake, history or share card: those are in the app. It saves nothing.
  • It does not predict anything. The phrase is an image to think about, and the English and Korean lines were written for Augura.
  • The Korean wording of the lines may differ from the explanations printed in old yutjeom booklets.
