Tea leaf reading (tasseography)

Read the symbols the leaves leave behind in the cup. This page has a free browser tool at the top and a guide to the Tea leaves mode in the Augura iPhone app (coming soon to the App Store). The browser tool is simpler than the app mode: there is no web version of the full mode.

Pour a cup of tea leaves here

Think of a question, then press Pour. A cup of tea leaves settles with three to five symbols in it, read from the rim to the bottom.

What this tool does

  • A pour places 3 to 5 different symbols out of 24 in the cup, with at least one in each of three places: the rim (soon), the side (later) and the bottom (far). The symbols are chosen first and then shown, and they are read from the rim down to the bottom as a short story.
  • Tea-leaf reading (tasseography) became popular in Europe in the 1700s and 1800s, when tea became an everyday drink. The meanings here are Augura's own, written in the spirit of that tradition, for reflection and not as prediction.
  • It does not read a real cup, a photo or coffee grounds. The browser version draws a flat picture of the cup and has no shake, no history and no share card (the app has them).
  • Everything runs in this page on your device with the browser's secure random generator. No request is sent after the page loads, no account is needed and nothing is saved: closing or reloading the tab clears it.

What it is

Tea-leaf reading (tasseography) finds shapes in the leaves left at the bottom of a cup. Augura shows a cup from above and lets 3 to 5 leaves settle in it. Each leaf takes the shape of a symbol.

How to use it

Think of a question, then tap Pour or shake your phone. The leaves settle and the symbols are listed below the cup. Tap a symbol to see it highlighted.

A sample cup

The three places in the cup

  • Rim (soon): what is close, in the coming days.
  • Side (later): what is on its way, in the coming weeks or months.
  • Bottom (far): what is deep or distant, and what it may become in time.

The 24 symbols

Each pour selects three to five different symbols, with at least one in every zone. All 24, with the app's own meanings:

  • Bird: News is on the wing. A message or an idea arrives; be ready to pass it on.
  • Key: Something can be opened that was stuck. Try the door you had given up on.
  • Anchor: Steadiness. Hold to what keeps you grounded and do not drift on a whim.
  • Ring: A circle closing: a promise, a return, or a cycle that completes itself.
  • Mountain: A big task, climbed one step at a time. The view is worth the effort.
  • Boat: A change of scene, or help coming across the water. Travel light.
  • Tree: Slow growth and good roots. What you plant now will shade someone later.
  • Star: Hope and a clear direction. Keep your eye on the one thing that guides you.
  • Heart: Warmth and affection, given or received. Do not hold back a kind word.
  • House: Home, shelter, and the people in it. Tend to your foundations.
  • Snake: A twist in the road, or a quiet worry. Look closely before you step.
  • Cloud: Doubt or confusion that will pass. Wait for the weather to change before you decide.
  • Sun: Clarity and good cheer. A time to be open, visible and generous.
  • Moon: Intuition and rest. Listen to what you already feel, and sleep on it.
  • Fish: Plenty and good luck, often from an unexpected source. Cast your net wide.
  • Flower: Something is coming into bloom. Be gentle with it and give it time.
  • Bridge: A way across a gap, between people or between phases of life. Cross it.
  • Candle: A small light in a dark hour. Share it; it will not burn down for giving.
  • Ladder: Steady progress upward, rung by rung. Check each step before you trust it.
  • Crown: Recognition for work well done. Accept it, and stay humble.
  • Knife: A clean cut: something should end or be trimmed away so the rest can thrive.
  • Wheel: Things are turning. What is low now will rise; what is high will not stay so.
  • Gate: A threshold. Opportunity stands open, but you have to step through.
  • Scattered dots: Many small things, none of them large. Sort them out, and do the little ones first.

How to read it

Where a leaf lands matters. Near the rim is what is close and soon. On the side is what comes later. At the bottom is what is far away or deep. Read the symbols from rim to bottom as a short story, and take it as food for thought, not a prediction.

Where it comes from

The habit grew popular in Europe in the 1700s and 1800s, when tea became an everyday drink. Similar customs exist with coffee grounds and wine. The meanings here are our own, written in the spirit of that tradition.

What it cannot do

  • It does not read a real cup: it cannot analyze a photo of your tea leaves, and it has no coffee grounds.
  • The browser tool draws a flat picture of the cup and has no shake, history or share card: those are in the app. It saves nothing.
  • It is not a reading by a person. The symbols are drawn by the app, and the meanings are Augura's own.
  • It does not predict anything. Take it as food for thought.
