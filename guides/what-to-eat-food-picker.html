What to eat? A random food picker

Pick one random everyday food in your browser, and see how the iPhone app's Food mode adds short, sourced nutrition context. This page has a free browser tool at the top and a guide to the Food mode in the Augura iPhone app (coming soon to the App Store). The browser tool is simpler than the app mode: there is no web version of the full mode.

Pick a food at random

Not sure what to eat? Choose which ingredients you eat, optionally one category, and press Pick a food. It names one food from a list of 27, not a meal or a recipe.

What this tool does

  • The list has 27 everyday foods, each in one of nine categories (fruit, vegetable, grain, legume, nuts and seeds, dairy, egg, fish, meat). Each eligible food is equally likely, and when more than one is left the food you just got is skipped.
  • The diet choice only sorts the categories: vegan has no dairy, egg, fish or meat; vegetarian adds dairy and egg; pescatarian adds fish; omnivore adds meat. It does not check recipes, packaged foods, allergies or medical needs, so it is not an allergy or safety check.
  • It names a food, not a meal or a recipe, and shows nothing about nutrition or health. It is no diet plan.
  • The foods are the list the Augura iPhone app uses, taken from U.S. government MyPlate food lists (myplate.gov).
  • Everything runs in this page on your device with the browser's secure random generator. No request is sent after the page loads, no account is needed and nothing is saved: closing or reloading the tab clears it.

What it is

Food draws one entry from a small offline catalogue of ordinary food ingredients. Fruit is one of its categories. Each card names the food, describes a relevant food group or nutrient role and links to the sources. These are educational summaries, not predictions about an individual person.

How to use it

  1. Choose a diet. In Options, choose vegan, vegetarian, pescatarian or omnivore ingredients. Narrow to Fruit or another category if you wish.
  2. Explore a food. Draw one food and read its short nutrition context. Sources explain the general nutrient role; portions, preparation and individual needs vary.
  3. Keep the same result. Open the sources or inspect a photograph without drawing again. History keeps the displayed food and summary; the next draw remains your choice.

Options offers vegan, vegetarian, pescatarian and omnivore ingredient filters, plus a food category. Vegetarian includes dairy and eggs; pescatarian also includes fish. Recipes, packaged foods, allergies and medical needs can differ, so these filters are not a safety certification. Plant-ingredient bread excludes animal ingredients.

How the draw works

Each eligible entry is equally likely, with the immediately previous entry set aside when another choice exists. Tap the draw button or shake the phone. History stores the displayed food, category, summary and source links. Reopening or sharing an entry does not draw again.

Where it comes from

The foods and their categories are the list the app carries offline. Each entry comes from a U.S. government MyPlate food list (myplate.gov), and the short nutrition notes the app shows next to each food are Augura's own wording of USDA, NIH and MedlinePlus material. The browser tool shows only the name and the category.

When a matching, reviewed plant photograph is available, its scientific name identifies the plant; it is not a photograph of a prepared meal. Optional photographs can need the existing Apple photo pack. Tap an available photograph for a full-screen view; compact credits open source and licence details. Nutrition context is independently worded from the linked USDA, NIH and MedlinePlus material.

What it cannot do

  • It does not plan a meal, suggest a recipe or know what is in your kitchen. It names one ingredient to start from.
  • It is not dietary, medical or allergy advice. The diet filters only sort the categories, and recipes, packaged foods and your own needs can differ.
  • It does not count calories or portions, and the browser tool shows no nutrition information at all.
  • The list is small and everyday: 27 foods, not restaurants, cuisines or dishes.
